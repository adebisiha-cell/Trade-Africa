What AfCFTA Means for Your Business

When you export fabric, clothing, or textile goods from Nigeria to another AfCFTA country, your buyer may pay a lower import duty — or none at all — on those goods.

This means:

  • Your buyer’s total cost for the product is lower
  • You become more price-competitive against non-African suppliers
  • You can keep a higher margin or lower your price to win more business

Example: A fabric buyer in Accra currently pays up to 20% import duty on Ankara fabric from Nigeria. Under AfCFTA, this could drop to near zero.

If you process and export agricultural products — palm oil, garri, spices, shea butter, or cocoa derivatives — AfCFTA works in the same way. Your buyer may get lower tariffs if your product meets the Rules of Origin requirements and you have the right export documents.

Agro exports have an extra requirement that fashion and textile exports do not: sanitary and phytosanitary compliance (see Agro Export Compliance). It protects the buyer’s market and Nigeria’s agricultural trade reputation, but adds another documentation step.

Who Qualifies?

You may qualify for AfCFTA benefits if your business is registered in Nigeria, your goods were produced or sufficiently processed there, you can document that, and you are exporting to another AfCFTA member country.
