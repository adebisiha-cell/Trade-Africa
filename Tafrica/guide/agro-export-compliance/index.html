Agro Export Compliance — NAQS, NAFDAC & Beyond

Agricultural and food exports from Nigeria have compliance steps beyond the Certificate of Origin. Here is what agro-processors and food exporters need to handle.

1. The Phytosanitary Certificate (NAQS)

The Nigeria Agricultural Quarantine Service (NAQS) certifies that agricultural exports are free from pests and diseases and meet international standards. A Phytosanitary Certificate is required for all plant and plant-product exports from Nigeria.

How to get one:

  1. Register as an exporter (free) at naqsportal.net
  2. Complete the application form (Agric Form 48) with details of the product and the importing country’s requirements
  3. Submit required documents: proforma invoice, packing list, and a sample of the product
  4. NAQS reviews the application and may request inspection and samples for laboratory analysis
  5. The certificate is sent to your registered email or collected in person

2. NAFDAC Registration (for Processed Foods)

If you’re exporting processed agricultural products — packaged garri, palm oil, spices, fruit juices, shea butter, and similar — you need NAFDAC registration in addition to the phytosanitary certificate. NAFDAC also issues Certificates of Analysis for registered products, which may be required as part of your export documentation.

3. Fumigation Certificate (where applicable)

Some commodities require a fumigation certificate to confirm the product has been treated to prevent pest infestation during transit. Check with NAQS whether your specific product requires this.

4. Veterinary Certificate (for animal products)

If your export includes animal or animal-derived products, the Department of Veterinary and Pest Control Services (DVPCS) issues the required International Veterinary Entry Document.

Important: Always check for product-specific export restrictions

Some agricultural commodities have separate export restrictions, regardless of AfCFTA eligibility. Nigeria currently restricts exports of raw, unprocessed shea nuts (kernels) to encourage local processing into higher-value shea butter. Cracking and drying the nuts does not remove that restriction; they must be genuinely processed into butter or oil.

Before sourcing goods or committing to a buyer, verify the current export status of your commodity with NEPC, NAQS, or Trade Africa. Rules can change, and exporting a restricted raw commodity can lead to seized goods or legal exposure. This is the kind of check we help community members make before they proceed.
